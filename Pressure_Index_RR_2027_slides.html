Two questions for every ball since 2022

How much was at stake, and how did the player do?

01

Who is winning?

A model trained on all 916 matches gives each side’s chance of winning before every ball. The last over of a chase is worked out exactly.

02

How much is at stake?

Stakes = how far the next ball could move that chance (typical ball = 1×). Pressure ball = top 20% (≥ 1.23×) or any knockout ball.

That ball carried 28× the stakes of a typical ball
03

How did he do?

Runs added = runs scored − what an average batter scores from the same spot; a wicket costs the runs it takes away. Bowlers mirror it.

Death overs: a four is about +2.5, a dot −1.5, a powerplay wicket −10
04

One rating per player

Runs added per 100 pressure balls, leaning on his usual level until his record is long (half and half at 200 balls). Each year back counts 20% less.

1 · Pressure definition

Pressure is where the next ball can swing the result

  • Pressure ball: the top 20% of balls by stakes (≥ 1.23× a typical ball), plus every knockout ball
  • Why stakes: it combines required rate, wickets, balls left, the target and how close the game is in one number
  • A dead chase is not pressure, however dramatic it looks: the chance barely moves
  • Rain: revised targets used; 11 matches under 10 overs a side left out

2 · Performance measurement

One formula, set against his usual level

rating = (Σ recency × runs added on pressure balls + 200 × usual level) ÷ (Σ recency + 200)
  • Runs added per ball: runs − expected runs from that spot − wicket cost; bowlers mirror it (byes not charged, run-outs not credited)
  • Usual level: the same sum on every other ball: the baseline the brief asks for
  • 200 balls: a short record leans on the usual level, so a hot streak cannot top the list
  • Lift = rating − usual level: does he rise or dip?
  • Recency: 2026 = 1.0, each year back 20% less (2022 = 0.2)

3 · Fair comparisons

Same situation, same yardstick, for every player

Sample size

50+ pressure balls in 4+ matches, 20+ in 2025–26. Short records lean on the usual level.

Indian players

A third of the bar (17 balls, 2 matches, 7 recent): the pack has no domestic cricket, only the IPL.

IPL and T20I together

Only top-level cricket: the IPL and T20Is between full members (806 matches). Each competition and season is its own average.

Pitches and opponents

T20Is recentred by host country and opponent each season: Bangladesh T20Is go at 7.9 an over, others 8.7.

Batting first

Knockout first-innings balls are pressure balls but count half: the other side still has to bat.

Built for 2027

2026 counts in full, each year back 20% less. Nobody is marked down for age; 35+ is flagged as a short-term buy.

5 · Where it is less certain

Honest limits, and the close calls

What the data cannot see

  • Fielding, pitch reports, batter-v-bowler match-ups
  • Injuries and form outside these matches
  • Domestic T20 (public SMAT record too patchy to rate fairly)
  • The 2022 and 2024 T20 World Cups

Modelling choices

  • First-innings pressure is harder to measure, so pressure balls lean towards chases
  • Batting under pressure is hard to predict at all (forward test 0.08)
  • Lift is a filter, because it repeats only weakly

Data used

The official pack, plus one public tournament

Taken as is: the data pack

  • ball_by_ball: every delivery: state, pressure, runs added
  • matches: knockouts, rain targets, venue country, results
  • competitions: IPL or T20I, season, top level
  • squad mapping: nationality, IPL team, RR squad
  • playing XIs: roles of RR’s retained players

Blended in: public data

  • ICC Men's T20 World Cup 2026: 49 matches, 11,555 balls from Cricsheet (CC BY)
  • Converted into the pack’s own format
  • Players matched to pack ids ball by ball on shared matches, then by name within a country
  • Treated exactly like any other T20I

Public facts for selection only

  • IPL 2027 retention lists (17 Nov 2026)
  • Retired and banned players
  • Birth dates (Emerging label only)
  • Nationality fixes for players with no T20I
